The Brazilian School System

The education system in Brazil is managed and administered by the Federal Government. Through the Ministry of Education (Ministerio de Educacao),the guiding principles for the development and coordination of all educational programmes are decided. All three levels of government-municipal, state and federal, are involved in the funding and provisions of all stages of public education. Pre-Primary is mandatory for Children ages 6-14 years and is absolutely free at public institutions. Secondary Educations and Primary Education are not mandatory but are still free at public institutions.

Most of Brazil's school institutions are free to the public but instructor numbers and funding is still much lower than most of the world. Brazil's average public school teacher salary is far lower than the U.S. average at R$104,800 BRL/year (about $20,000 USD/year). This is much lower compared to the average for teachers in the US at $76,221 USD. At almost 4x less, the massive pay gap is only a part of a larger sect of problems stemming from lower amounts of available funding. This lack of money has also led to less poeple being available to teach along with more expeirenced teachers leaving.

The proportion of 15-to-24 year old men and woman neither in employment nor education or training peaked in 2020 the ratio of women being 31.2% and the ratio of men being 20.9%. The proportion of women was noticably higher than that of men and continues to be the case. Teens and young adults who were unable to finish school and work often live in poverty and have to turn to crime to survive.

Most of the poeple who enroll in school are bewtween 6 and 14 years old.This means that they are likley entering primary school. There are less children entering pre-primary school at ages 3-5 and even less erolling into secondary school at ages 15-19. This low inital level of pre-primary school enrollments can lead to many kids who begin primary school starting at a disadvantage. This disadvantage can then further lead to a lower number of secondary school enrollments. This simple cascade negativly impacts the number of students attending the higher levels of education and over time this number of non-enrolling students who won't be able to find good jobs will multpily until eventually most children wont be able to finish school because thier parents didn't and werent able to make a livable wage. This will inevitably lead to economic collapse.
